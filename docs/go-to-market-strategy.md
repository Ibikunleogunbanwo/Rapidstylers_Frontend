# RapidStylers go-to-market strategy

Prepared: October 11, 2026.

## Launch approach

Launch in stages. Start with one city and a focused set of services, establish reliable customer and stylist journeys, then expand based on completed bookings and service quality.

Calgary is a proposed starting market, not a confirmed decision. The launch city, initial categories, budget, owners and dates still need to be agreed. Timing depends on readiness rather than a fixed public launch date.

## Step 1: Close launch readiness

- Complete the outstanding provider-workflow and browser-compatibility release gates.
- Confirm GitHub CI and production deployment for the launch candidate.
- Verify registration, discovery, booking, payment, completion and payout end to end.
- Verify cancellation, refunds, disputes, upload ownership and logout/session behaviour.
- Prepare support coverage, refund procedures, escalation contacts and incident handling.

**Exit criteria:** Both customer and stylist journeys pass end to end; release evidence is recorded; support can handle launch issues.

## Step 2: Choose the launch market

- Select one city and a small set of service categories.
- Define the service area and available appointment times.
- Make prices, travel fees and cancellation terms clear before booking.
- Establish how much demand the initial stylist group can handle.

**Exit criteria:** Customers in the selected area can find available professionals at clear prices. Confirm the city and categories before announcing availability.

## Step 3: Recruit and onboard stylists

- Personally onboard a manageable initial group.
- Verify profiles, portfolios and payout setup.
- Check availability and service coverage.
- Explain booking acceptance, customer communication, cancellations and disputes.
- Agree who will support stylists during their first appointments.

**Exit criteria:** Active stylists with bookable availability cover the initial area and categories. Profile counts alone do not establish supply.

## Step 4: Run an invitation-only pilot

- Invite a manageable customer group matched to available stylist capacity.
- Support first bookings closely and collect feedback after appointments.
- Record booking failures, cancellations, complaints and payout delays.
- Fix recurring issues before increasing invitation volume.
- Keep a weekly pilot review with named owners for unresolved issues.

**Exit criteria:** Real appointments complete successfully, payouts reconcile, and recurring problems are resolved. Agree numerical pilot targets before invitations begin.

## Step 5: Open a local public launch

- Publish clear pages for the available services and locations.
- Use actual stylist work with permission and accurate attribution.
- Promote through participating stylists and relevant local partnerships.
- Keep support visible and communicate service coverage honestly.
- Measure whether new customers can book without hands-on assistance.

**Exit criteria:** Customers can discover and book independently; stylist availability and support capacity keep pace with demand.

## Step 6: Test customer acquisition

- Test one acquisition channel at a time.
- Set a capped budget, measurement period and stop criteria before spending.
- Track completed bookings alongside signups and visits.
- Compare acquisition cost with platform contribution margin after payment costs, refunds and promotions.
- Expand channels only when results justify the spend.

**Exit criteria:** Acquisition costs are sustainable against margin and repeat booking behaviour. Increased traffic alone is not a reason to scale.

## Step 7: Expand gradually

- Add service categories or nearby areas where demand and available supply justify expansion.
- Confirm onboarding, payout and support capacity before expanding.
- Monitor whether expansion reduces acceptance rates or service quality in the existing market.

**Exit criteria:** Existing service quality and support remain steady while the new area or category demonstrates reliable completed bookings.

## Weekly scorecard

| Metric | What it tells us |
|---|---|
| Active stylists with bookable availability | Whether customers have usable supply |
| Search-to-booking conversion | Whether discovery leads to bookings |
| Booking request acceptance | Whether supply matches customer requests |
| Completed bookings | Whether the marketplace delivers its service |
| Cancellations and disputes | Where reliability or expectations break down |
| Repeat customers | Whether customers return |
| Platform margin after refunds, payment costs and promotions | Whether transactions are sustainable |
| Support response time | Whether customers and stylists receive timely help |
| Payout reliability | Whether professionals are paid correctly and on time |

Use existing admin data where available. Until reporting contracts are implemented, maintain a consistent weekly scorecard with defined periods and denominators. Avoid treating missing data as zero.

## Decisions before the pilot

- Confirm the launch city and initial service categories.
- Set the initial stylist capacity and customer invitation target.
- Assign owners for onboarding, support, technical readiness and acquisition.
- Agree the pilot success measures and stop/rollback criteria.
- Set an initial budget and spending approval boundaries.
- Select a pilot date only after readiness is confirmed.
